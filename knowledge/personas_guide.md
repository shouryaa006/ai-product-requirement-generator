# Personas and User Profile Design

User personas are semi-fictional representations of your target users based on real data and qualitative research. They help product teams build empathy and design features tailored to specific user behaviors and needs.

## Structured Components of a Persona

Every high-quality persona should have:

1. **Name and Photo/Avatar**: Creating a realistic persona (e.g., "Aisha, Sophomore Computer Science Major").
2. **Role/Title**: Their official or functional title (e.g., "Undergraduate Learner", "Freelance Peer Tutor").
3. **Demographics & Context**: Background details relevant to their product usage (e.g., age, device preferences, technical literacy).
4. **Key Goals**: What they are trying to accomplish by using your product.
5. **Major Pain Points**: The bottlenecks, frustrations, or friction points they face in their current workflow.

## Example Personas

### Persona 1: Aisha - The Struggling CS Student
- **Role**: Second-year Undergraduate Learner.
- **Goals**: Pass data structures and algorithms, find affordable tutoring on campus, and study with peers.
- **Pain Points**: Private tutors cost $50+/hour (unaffordable), university-provided free drop-in centers have hours that clash with her schedule, and classmates are too busy to help.

### Persona 2: Marcus - The High-Achieving Tutor
- **Role**: Junior Computer Science Major / Freelance Peer Tutor.
- **Goals**: Earn extra income, help peers succeed, build resume credentials, and choose his own working hours.
- **Pain Points**: Difficulty advertising his availability to students who need help, unsafe or unpredictable meet-up locations, and chasing clients for payment.
