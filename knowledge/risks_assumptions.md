# Product Risks and Assumptions

Product development involves substantial uncertainty. High-performing teams identify, document, and mitigate risks and assumptions early in the product lifecycle.

## Understanding the Difference

- **Assumption**: A statement or belief accepted as true or certain to happen, without concrete proof (e.g., "Students have enough disposable income to pay peer tutors").
- **Risk**: A future uncertain event that, if it occurs, will have a negative impact on the product's timeline, cost, quality, or adoption (e.g., "The university might ban peer-to-peer monetary transactions on campus").

## Common Categories of Risks

1. **Feasibility Risk (Can we build it?)**: Technical barriers, missing developer skills, or library incompatibilities.
2. **Usability Risk (Can users figure out how to use it?)**: Complex user interfaces or confusing navigation.
3. **Value Risk (Will they choose to use or buy it?)**: Users do not care about the features or refuse to pay for the service.
4. **Viability Risk (Does this solution fit our business constraints?)**: Legal, regulatory, financial, or security hurdles.

## Managing Risks and Assumptions

- **Mitigation Strategy**: The practical action taken to reduce the probability or impact of a risk.
- **Assumption Mapping**: Prioritizing assumptions based on how critical they are to the product's success and how little evidence is currently available. High-criticality, low-evidence assumptions must be tested first through MVP experiments.
