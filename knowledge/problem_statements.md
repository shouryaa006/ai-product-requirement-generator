# Product Problem Statements and Discovery

A strong problem statement is the anchor of any successful product. It defines *who* has the problem, *what* the problem is, *where/when* it occurs, and *why* it matters.

## Crafting a Great Problem Statement

A well-formulated problem statement should follow a structure such as:
> **"Our [Target Audience] experiences [Core Problem] when [Context/Situation], resulting in [Negative Impact/Emotion/Cost]. This is a critical issue because [Business/Value Impact]."**

### Example Problem Statements

1. **For Student-Tutor matching**: "On-campus college students experience difficulty finding qualified, verified peer tutors for advanced courses during midterm week, resulting in academic stress and lower course grades. This is critical because peer tutoring increases retention, and existing general platforms are too expensive and not aligned with university syllabi."
2. **For Recipe-to-Grocery app**: "Busy working parents experience frustration when trying to convert weekly meal recipes into organized, store-sorted grocery lists, resulting in wasted time and duplicate grocery purchases. This matters because families lose up to 15 hours a month to unorganized shopping, and existing shopping apps do not support recipe-based bulk import."

## Connection to Discovery

Product discovery is the process of validating that a problem exists and is worth solving before writing any code. Discovery techniques include:
- **User Interviews**: Talking directly to target users about their current workarounds and pain points.
- **Surveys**: Gathering quantitative data on the prevalence of the problem.
- **Competitive Analysis**: Evaluating how existing solutions fall short.
